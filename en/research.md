At the center of every massive galaxy lies a supermassive black hole with a mass of millions to billions of solar masses. Our laboratory studies the physics of these black holes and their environments through **large-scale numerical simulations** and **observations with X-ray astronomy satellites**.

## Large-scale numerical simulations
We model accretion flows, radiation, and outflows around supermassive black holes using supercomputers, and predict the observable X-ray spectra with radiative transfer calculations.

## X-ray observations of supermassive black holes
X-rays directly probe the hot plasma in the immediate vicinity of black holes. We analyze data from X-ray satellites such as XRISM, NuSTAR, XMM-Newton, and Chandra to investigate the circumnuclear structure and growth history of supermassive black holes.

## Bridging theory and observation
By directly comparing simulation-based models with observational data, we aim to build a unified picture of active galactic nuclei.
