We study supermassive black holes at the centers of galaxies through large-scale numerical simulations and X-ray satellite data analysis. No prior experience in physics or programming is required; curiosity about space and computing is all you need.

## Prerequisites
- **Physics / Mathematics**: Basics are covered in the seminar. Knowledge at the level of "Mathematics 1" is sufficient.
- **Programming**: Beginners are welcome. We start with Python from scratch in the first semester.
- **English**: Research papers are in English, but we begin with Japanese materials and build up gradually.

## A typical third year

| Term | Activities |
|---|---|
| Spring | Introduction to Python, basics of black holes and X-ray astronomy, reading the lab's papers |
| Summer | Small independent project (simple simulation or data analysis) |
| Fall | Hands-on analysis of X-ray satellite data, survey of topics, choosing a graduation research theme |

## Examples of graduation research topics
- Measuring gas motion around a supermassive black hole from XRISM spectra of an active galactic nucleus
- Simulating gas accretion onto a black hole and predicting the resulting X-ray features
- Statistical analysis of X-ray data of many galaxies to study black hole growth

## Skills you will gain
- Programming and data analysis with Python
- Linux and supercomputer usage
- Reading papers, giving presentations, and scientific discussion

These skills are directly useful for careers in IT and data science. Students wishing to go on to graduate school are also supported.
